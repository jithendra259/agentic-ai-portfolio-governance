\begin{abstract}
Institutional portfolio managers face a structural problem that standard
tail-risk optimizers do not address: during financial crises, the assets
most exposed to forced liquidation are precisely those in which multiple
large institutions hold overlapping positions. Standard Conditional
Value-at-Risk (CVaR) controls tail losses within individual rebalancing
windows but contains no mechanism to penalize the hidden cross-sector
linkages created by common institutional ownership. This paper introduces
the Graph-Regularized CVaR Agentic Portfolio Governance System
(G-CVaR-APGS), a five-agent supervisory framework in which a persistent
MongoDB blackboard coordinates deterministic agents responsible for data
ingestion, composite instability detection, bipartite contagion graph
construction, graph-penalized CVaR optimization, and explainable
attribution auditing. The core optimizer penalizes each asset's weight
proportionally to its eigenvector centrality in the institutional
holdings graph, scaled by a sigmoid adaptive trust parameter
$\lambda_t$ that activates selectively when the composite instability
index $I_t$ crosses a calibrated crisis threshold.Human-in-the-Loop
(HITL) governance layer intercepts crisis and high-turnover windows,
offering Approve, Reject, or Constrain overrides with full XAI audit
trails.
 
The framework was tested against 11 GICS sectors universes, 218 stocks, and
552 rolling windows for the period between 2005 and 2025 — covering the
Global Financial Crisis of 2008, the COVID-19 shock of 2020, and the rate
hike cycle of 2022 — and delivered a mean reduction in CVaR@95\% of
25.9\% and a maximum reduction in drawdown.
The mean Sharpe ratio of 0.646 represents a
66.5\% improvement over equal weighting. G-CVaR trails Standard CVaR by
0.007 Sharpe points ($p = 0.052$, Wilcoxon), a deliberate consequence of
the graph penalty redistributing weight away from systemically
concentrated positions — a risk-governance trade-off, not a modeling
failure. Walk-forward validation, four-condition ablation, Monte Carlo
stress testing across 10,500 synthetic paths, and cross-universe
robustness analysis confirm that crisis-regime performance improvements
are attributable to the adaptive graph penalty rather than parameter
overfitting.
\end{abstract}
